# Consistency between transformed views in fully supervised training: prior work bearing on FCT's novelty

Scope: fully supervised classification and segmentation that train on several transformed views of each image and add an agreement term between the views' predictions. Semi-supervised methods appear only as brief background. FCT is the method under review: three deterministic views (upright, horizontal flip, vertical flip), each supervised at weight 1/3; per-pixel soft-BCE consistency toward detached targets, lambda = 0.5; pull (a) moves the flipped views toward the upright view, with a "lag-or-margin" ground-truth weight; pull (b) moves the upright view toward the flipped view closest to the label, only where that view is more accurate; the test uses the upright view only.

Venues were checked against primary sources: Crossref DOIs, the NeurIPS, PMLR and CVF proceedings indexes, ECVA, and the arXiv API (author comments). Where only an arXiv record exists, the work is labelled a preprint.

## 1. Which works train fully supervised models on several transformed views per image and add a consistency term between their predictions?

### Takeaway
Many do, from 2016 to 2026, in classification, natural-image segmentation, domain generalization (DG) and medical segmentation. For polyps there is a direct precedent. Segmentation Consistency Training (SCT; Torpmann-Hagen et al., IEEE ISM 2022) trains on Kvasir-SEG with a label-aware consistency loss between an unaugmented view and an augmented view, the augmentations including 90-degree rotations, and reports gains on unseen ETIS, CVC-ClinicDB and EndoCV2020. "Several supervised views plus a cross-view agreement term" is therefore an established recipe. What varies between works is the choice of views, the symmetry or direction of the loss, and whether and how the label enters.

### Cited Findings
**Medical and polyp segmentation (fully supervised)**
- SCT (Torpmann-Hagen, Thambawita, Glette, Halvorsen, Riegler; 2022 IEEE International Symposium on Multimedia, DOI 10.1109/ism55400.2022.00012) proposes "Consistency Training ... based on maximizing models' prediction consistency across augmented and unaugmented data in order to facilitate better out-of-distribution generalization". Its Segmentation Inconsistency Loss (SIL) "considers the differences between pairs of augmented and unaugmented predictions and labels". It is evaluated on polyp segmentation with DeepLabV3+, FPN, UNet and TriUNet trained on Kvasir-SEG and tested on Etis-LaribDB, CVC-ClinicDB and EndoCV2020. — [arXiv 2205.15428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15428); [venue DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ism55400.2022.00012); [full text PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15428)
- Bortsova et al. (MICCAI 2019) is a Siamese, transformation-consistency method (random elastic deformations, identity with 50% probability). It includes "SupTC", in which the consistency loss is applied only to labeled images, with both branches also supervised. On chest X-rays SupTC beats supervised training with the same augmentation in 24 of 25 experiments (for example 74.2 to 76.4 mIoU with 5 labeled images, and 90.6 to 90.9 with 100). — [ar5iv 1911.01218](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01218); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-32226-7_90)
- Causality-inspired single-source DG (Ouyang et al., IEEE TMI 2023) makes two augmented views per image (GIN and IPA). It applies a segmentation loss (CE plus Dice) to each view and adds a KL consistency term between them, lambda_div = 10. — [ar5iv 2111.12525](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.12525); [TMI DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2022.3224067)
- Consisaug (Zhou, Shen, Liu; MLMI 2023, LNCS) is for polyp detection, not segmentation. It uses an EMA teacher-student, horizontal flips, JS divergence on class scores and MSE on box offsets, with total loss = the fully supervised detector loss plus the consistency loss. — [ar5iv 2404.11355](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2404.11355); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-45676-3_7)
- Recent polyp work adds consistency or self-distillation that is not between transformed views. PRISM (Healthcare Technology Letters 2025) uses an EMA-teacher self-distillation, evaluated on five centres plus an unseen dataset (Dice 0.81) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1049/htl2.70050)). DCSD (arXiv 2025) is a confidence-weighted self-distillation from the previous iteration ([arXiv 2507.10490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10490)). PlutoNet (Authorea preprint 2024) uses "decoder consistency training" between two decoders ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.22541/au.173139326.64378195/v1)). "Progressive contour-consistent self-distillation for polyp segmentation" appeared in Neurocomputing in 2026 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2026.134234)); its method could not be checked (see Gaps).

**Natural-image segmentation (fully supervised)**
- SHADE (Zhao et al., ECCV 2022) applies Style Consistency, a JS divergence between the pixel-wise posteriors of a source image and its style-hallucinated version. CE is applied to both views (weight 1/2 each), lambda_SC = 10, plus a feature-level Retrospection Consistency. — [ar5iv 2204.02548](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.02548); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-19815-1_31); [ECVA](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/86_ECCV_2022_paper.php)
- DRPC (Yue et al., ICCV 2019) randomizes synthetic images with real styles and "enforce[s] pyramid consistency across different 'stylized' images and within an image", training only on source data (GTA or SYNTHIA to Cityscapes, BDDS and Mapillary). — [arXiv 1909.00889](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00889); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/iccv.2019.00219)
- Learning Equivariant Segmentation with Instance-Unique Querying (Wang, Liang, Liu; NeurIPS 2022) trains query-based instance segmenters with a fully supervised transformation-equivariance loss under flips and crops. The loss uses the ground-truth masks of the transformed view, lambda = 3. — [ar5iv 2210.00911](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.00911); [NeurIPS 2022 index](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022)
- Weakly supervised background: Laradji et al. (WACV 2021) propose a consistency-based loss "that encourages the output predictions to be consistent with spatial transformations of the input images", with point labels. — [arXiv 2007.02180](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02180); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/wacv48630.2021.00250)

**Classification: robustness, DG and regularization (fully supervised)**
- AugMix (Hendrycks et al.) minimizes L(p_orig, y) + lambda JS(p_orig; p_augmix1; p_augmix2), with JS taken to the mixture M of the three predictions and CE on the original view only. — [ar5iv 1912.02781](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.02781)
- RandConv (Xu et al., ICLR 2021 per the authors' arXiv comment) uses three random-convolution views and L = L_task(view 1) + lambda sum_j KL(y_j || mean), lambda = 10. — [ar5iv 2007.13003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.13003); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13003)
- FACT (Xu et al., CVPR 2021) is amplitude-mix augmentation plus a "dual-formed consistency loss called co-teacher regularization between the predictions induced from original and augmented images". It applies CE to both views and uses two KL terms against an EMA teacher. — [ar5iv 2105.11120](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.11120); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr46437.2021.01415)
- R-Drop (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2021) "minimizes the bidirectional KL-divergence between the output distributions of two sub models sampled by dropout". The views come from dropout, not from input transforms. — [arXiv 2106.14448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14448); [NeurIPS 2021 index](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021)
- DDGSD (Xu and Liu, AAAI 2019) is self-distillation "between different distorted versions of same training data", learning "consistent global feature distributions and posterior distributions (class probabilities) across these distorted versions". — [Crossref record and abstract, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33015565)
- Botev, Bauer and De (DeepMind; arXiv 2022) compare objectives with K augmentations per input (average of losses, of probabilities, of logits). They add an explicit regularizer: the symmetrized KL (Jeffreys) between predictions on pairs of augmentations, lambda = 1. — [ar5iv 2203.03304](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.03304)
- DAC (Yang et al., AISTATS 2023) contrasts DA-ERM (all augmented copies supervised with their labels) with DAC (supervised loss on the originals plus a consistency penalty between original and augmented outputs). It analyses both theoretically and runs experiments. — [ar5iv 2202.12230](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.12230); [PMLR v206 index](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v206/)
- GJS (Englesson and Azizpour, NeurIPS 2021) is a generalized JS divergence over the one-hot label and predictions on M - 1 augmented views (M = 3). It is used for noisy labels. — [ar5iv 2105.04522](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.04522); [NeurIPS 2021 index](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021)
- KDCL (Guo et al., CVPR 2020) is online distillation between peer students, each fed a differently distorted copy of the image ("we randomly sample images with the same data augmentation policy for each sub-network"), toward a shared soft target built from their logits. — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf); [CVF page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.html)
- Deep Mutual Learning (Zhang et al., CVPR 2018) is the peer-network precedent: "an ensemble of students learn collaboratively and teach each other". — [CVF page](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Zhang_Deep_Mutual_Learning_CVPR_2018_paper.html); [arXiv 1706.00384](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.00384)
- SLA (Lee, Hwang, Shin; ICML 2020) supervises input transformations (rotations) through joint labels in fully supervised training. Its "aggregated inference ... combines the predictions from different augmentations", and a self-distillation transfers that aggregated inference into "a single (faster) inference". — [arXiv 1910.05872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05872); [PMLR v119 index](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/)
- Robustness-oriented relatives:
  - Stability training (Zheng et al., CVPR 2016) stabilizes outputs against small input distortions ([arXiv 1604.04326](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.04326); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr.2016.485)).
  - Yang, Wang and Heinze-Deml (NeurIPS 2019) show that "invariance-inducing regularizers" with worst-case spatial transformations improve accuracy and spatial robustness ([arXiv 1906.11235](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11235); [NeurIPS 2019 index](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019)).
  - Tack et al. (AAAI 2022) force "the predictive distributions after attacking from two different augmentations of the same instance to be similar" ([arXiv 2103.04623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04623)).

**Multi-view supervision without a consistency term (controls for FCT's views)**
- Batch augmentation (Hoffer et al., CVPR 2020) replicates "instances of samples within the same batch with different data augmentations" and finds it "acts as a regularizer". — [arXiv 1901.09335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09335); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr42600.2020.00815)
- Fort et al. (arXiv 2021) find that "drawing multiple samples per image consistently enhances the test accuracy ... even if different numbers of augmentations per image perform the same number of parameter updates and gradient evaluations". — [arXiv 2105.13343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13343)
- MaxUp (Gong et al.; CVPR 2021, titled "MaxUp: Lightweight Adversarial Training with Data Augmentation Improves Neural Network Training") draws several augmented copies and minimizes "the maximum, or worst case loss over the augmented data". The label selects a view here, but the worst one, and only for supervision. — [arXiv 2002.09024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.09024); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/cvpr46437.2021.00250)

**Theory**
- DAC proves that consistency is more sample-efficient than DA-ERM for label-invariant augmentations in linear regression, and extends this to misspecified augmentations and neural networks. — [arXiv 2202.12230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12230)
- Chen, Dobriban and Lee (JMLR, per the arXiv comment) cast augmentation as "an averaging operation over the orbits of a certain group" that reduces variance ([arXiv 1907.10905](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.10905)). Lyle et al. (arXiv 2020) compare data augmentation with feature averaging under invariance ([arXiv 2005.00178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.00178)).

**Semi-supervised and test-time background (brief)**
- In semi-supervised segmentation, Grubišić et al. report "clear advantages of (1) one-way consistency, (2) perturbing only the student branch". Their one-way variant prevents "the backward pass through the unperturbed instance", which is the stop-gradient pattern FCT's pull (a) uses. — [arXiv 2106.07075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07075)
- The draft already cites the Pi-model and temporal ensembling, Mean Teacher, UDA, FixMatch, CCT, CPS, UniMatch, TCSM ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tnnls.2020.2995319)), UA-MT and AC-MT (see /home/user/BACFR/paper/references.bib). These use consistency on unlabeled data and do not bear on the fully supervised question.
- Test-time equivariance: Mao et al. (ICML 2023, per the arXiv comment) restore feature equivariance at inference for robustness. This sits on the inference side, unlike FCT's training-only loss. — [arXiv 2212.06079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06079)

### Inferences
- FCT's ingredient (1), several views each supervised with its correspondingly transformed label, is standard: batch augmentation, multi-sample augmentation, Botev et al.'s "average of losses", CE on both views in FACT and SHADE, the segmentation loss on both views in Ouyang et al., and both branches supervised in Bortsova's SupTC. Using the deterministic flip orbit {id, h, v} rather than random draws is a design choice, not a contribution.
- FCT's ingredient (2), a cross-view agreement term under full supervision, is also established. In segmentation it is established specifically: Bortsova SupTC in MICCAI 2019, SCT for polyps in 2022, SHADE and DRPC for DG segmentation, Ouyang et al. for medical DG, and the NeurIPS 2022 equivariant instance segmentation.
- Mapping geometric views back to a common frame before comparing them (equivariance) appears in Bortsova (an inverse-transform layer) and in the NeurIPS 2022 work (f(g(I)) ≈ g(f(I))). SCT compares change maps in the image frame instead (see Q2).

### Gaps
- The method of "Progressive contour-consistent self-distillation for polyp segmentation" (Neurocomputing 2026) could not be checked: Crossref has no abstract and no open full text was found. A reviewer might cite it, so its full text should be checked before submission.
- A master's thesis by SCT's first author (UiO DUO repository) came up in a search but was not read. No journal extension of SCT was found in Crossref.
- No 2023-2026 paper was found that applies cross-view consistency (rather than temporal or EMA self-distillation) in fully supervised polyp segmentation. The search covered web, arXiv and Crossref but was not exhaustive (Google Scholar was not queried directly).

## 2. For each close work: views, symmetric loss vs stop-gradient target, which view is the target, ground-truth use, direction reversal, and reported generalization or OOD gains

### Takeaway
Most close works use a symmetric, label-free agreement term (AugMix, R-Drop, SHADE, Botev, Bortsova) or a fixed-direction pull toward the clean view or a teacher (FACT's a2o, Consisaug, the semi-supervised one-way variants). The label enters a consistency target in only three places found:
- GJS puts the one-hot label inside the JS mixture.
- SCT uses the label to define the "expected change" between views.
- KDCL uses the ground-truth CE to choose or fit the soft target among peer students that saw differently distorted inputs. KDCL-Naive picks the student with the lowest CE, which is a sample-level "pull toward the best view".

FACT already has two directed pulls between the original and augmented views (original-to-augmented and augmented-to-original), and its ablation finds both better than either alone. But FACT's directions are always on, use an EMA teacher, and ignore the label. No work was found that decides the direction of the pull per pixel by comparing each view's error with the label.

- More SCT detail:
  - SCT's SIL is computed in the image frame without mapping predictions back. The authors note it is "largely agnostic to the augmentation being performed".
  - Under a 90-degree rotation it treats a prediction as consistent "so long as the pixels corresponding to the polyps are rotated, and the incorrectly classified pixels remain unchanged".
  - As future work they propose replacing the expected change a⊕y with ŷ⊕T(ŷ). This is an equivariance-to-own-prediction form, closer to FCT's mapped-back consistency, but they did not evaluate it.
  - Source: [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15428)
- SCT adds the per-batch IoU weighting because plain addition "exhibited some degree of instability ... consistent, but also consistently predicting artifacts". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15428)
- Bortsova et al. compare their differentiable alignment with a non-differentiable variant that would treat one branch's output as a fixed target. They argue the differentiable version avoids forcing predictions toward possibly wrong targets. This is the opposite design choice to FCT's detached targets. — [ar5iv 1911.01218](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01218)
- KDCL motivates GT-aware targets by noting that peer predictions can be "diverse, conflicting with each other and even the ground truth", and generates "identical soft target for all students fed with similar distorted images" to improve invariance to input perturbations. — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)
- In GJS the label sits inside the target, but its weight π1 is fixed. The authors conclude that "using the mean of two predictions in the JS loss does not improve performance" while the consistency term "significantly helps". — [ar5iv 2105.04522](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.04522)
- In Ouyang et al., source-domain test Dice drops slightly relative to ERM (about 2.0 for abdominal CT and about 1.0 for prostate), while target-domain Dice rises. — [ar5iv 2111.12525](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.12525)

### Inferences
- SCT's label-awareness is close to FCT's in spirit (the label decides what counts as an "error" of consistency) but differs in mechanism:
  - SCT's term is symmetric and penalizes any prediction change that the label change does not explain.
  - FCT's terms are directed, have detached targets, and are gated per pixel by which view is closer to the label. FCT also maps the views back to the upright frame.
- FACT is the structural precedent for "two pulls". Its a2o term is FCT's pull (a) without the weight, using an EMA teacher instead of the detached current prediction. Its o2a term pulls the original view toward the augmented teacher everywhere, while FCT's pull (b) does so only where a flipped view is more accurate. FACT's single-direction rows (83.67, 83.84) still beat AM-only (83.44). FCT reports that no single-pull variant beats vanilla, so FCT's evidence that both pulls are necessary is stronger than FACT's, on a different task.
- An unweighted pair of detached soft-BCE pulls moves the logit of each view toward the other by (p_f − p_u). This is my own derivation from the stated loss. It has the same sign and fixed point as a symmetric consistency loss such as R-Drop's bidirectional KL, AugMix's JS or Bortsova's two-branch gradients. A "both pulls, no label weighting" variant is therefore essentially symmetric consistency, and the specific novelty lies in the label gating and the privileged role of the upright view.
- KDCL-Naive is the only found precedent in which the ground truth decides which view becomes the target. It works at sample level, across separate peer networks and random distortions, in classification, and pulls all students toward the selected one. FCT works per pixel, within one network, across deterministic flips, and gives the test-time (upright) view a special asymmetric role.

### Gaps
- The released code for SCT, FACT, SHADE and RandConv was not checked, so stop-gradient handling where the papers are silent (SHADE, RandConv, Ouyang et al., Consisaug) is unknown.
- AugMix's lambda value and the exact TRADES and Stability-training formulas were not re-verified in full text and are not used in the table.
- AugMix's ICLR 2020 venue (as listed in the draft's references.bib) could not be re-confirmed in this session: the OpenReview API returned no data. Only its arXiv record (1912.02781) was confirmed. The same applies to RandConv's ICLR 2021 venue, which rests on the authors' arXiv comment.
- It is not clear whether FCT's "unweighted" variant keeps the label-based selection inside pull (b) ("best flipped view ... only where more accurate"). If it does, label awareness is intrinsic to FCT's "core", and the KDCL comparison becomes central. If it does not, the core reduces to symmetric consistency. - Dual Student (ICCV 2019, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/iccv.2019.00683)) and SwitOKD were surfaced as dynamic-direction schemes, but their mechanisms were not verified from full text, so they are not used here. Both are semi-supervised or online-KD settings in any case.

## 3. Is there prior evidence separating the effect of deterministic multi-view supervision from the effect of the consistency term?

### Takeaway
Yes, but only for random augmentations and mostly in classification. Botev et al. hold K-view loss averaging fixed and show that an added symmetric-KL consistency term improves accuracy further. FACT, SHADE and GJS show gains of the consistency term over CE on both views. Two caveats cut the other way:
- Multi-sample augmentation alone also helps, even at matched compute (Fort et al.; Hoffer et al.).
- Several well-known comparisons (AugMix, SCT, Bortsova) do not control for the number of views. SCT's authors flag this themselves.

No study was found that separates the two effects for a deterministic flip orbit in segmentation, and none at all for polyps. FCT's "views only" control would be new evidence there.

### Cited Findings
- Botev et al., with the number of views held fixed: with K_train = 16 (CIFAR-100) or 8 (ImageNet), the average of losses gives 75.0% / 79.3% / 81.3% and the added Jeffreys regularizer gives 75.9% / 80.1% / 81.9% (WRN-16-4 / NF-ResNet-101 / NFNet-F0). The authors also report that the best-performing objective has the most similar predictions across augmentations. — [ar5iv 2203.03304](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.03304)
- FACT, with CE on both views in all variants: AM only 83.44 → AM + co-teacher 84.51 PACS average. Single directions give 83.67 (a2o) and 83.84 (o2a); both directions without a teacher give 83.71. — [ar5iv 2105.11120](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.11120)
- SHADE: training on hallucinated samples with CE (SHM only) 35.68 → adding Style Consistency 39.67 mean mIoU on unseen real domains. — [ar5iv 2204.02548](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.02548)
- GJS: with the label plus the mean of two views but no consistency term, accuracy is 68.7 (below the single view's 71.0); adding the consistency term gives 74.3 (CIFAR-100, 40% noise). — [ar5iv 2105.04522](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.04522)
- DAC vs DA-ERM, same augmented data:
  - DAC beats DA-ERM at every tested number of augmentations, most at 1 (67.92 vs 70.06) and least at 15 (69.30 vs 70.31).
  - With 10 transformation types, DA-ERM wins (69.66 vs 68.95). The authors attribute this to enforcing consistency where labels have changed.
  - DAC with lambda = 0 (originals only) reaches 62.82.
  - Note that DAC's comparison is "views supervised" vs "original supervised + consistency", not "views supervised" vs "views supervised + consistency".
  - Source: [ar5iv 2202.12230](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.12230)
- Equivariant instance segmentation: standard augmentation 35.5 AP (35.3 without augmentation) vs the equivariance loss 36.5 AP with the same flip and crop transforms. — [ar5iv 2210.00911](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.00911)
- Consisaug: flip augmentation alone raises mAP50 from 0.501 to 0.510, the consistency method alone gives 0.515, and both give 0.540. — [ar5iv 2404.11355](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2404.11355)
- RandConv: the consistency term helps on digits (71.19→72.88) but lowers PACS average accuracy (70.53→69.62) while helping Sketch. Consistency on top of multi-view augmentation is not uniformly beneficial. — [ar5iv 2007.13003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2007.13003)
- AugMix: JSD lowers CIFAR-10-C error from 17.0 to 14.7 (Random AutoAugment) and from 13.1 to 11.2 (AugmentAndMix). The fetched text does not say whether the no-JSD rows also use three forward views. If they use one, views and consistency are confounded in this ablation. — [ar5iv 1912.02781](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.02781)
- Multi-view supervision alone helps. Fort et al. report gains from several augmentation samples per image "even if different numbers of augmentations per image perform the same number of parameter updates and gradient evaluations" ([arXiv 2105.13343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13343)). Hoffer et al. report that batch augmentation "acts as a regularizer" ([arXiv 1901.09335](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09335)).
- SCT does not control for the number of views and says so: "as it can be argued that since Consistency Training implicitly increases the batch size, the experiments should ideally be repeated across a range of batch sizes". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15428)
- Bortsova et al. note that SupTC often had similar or higher supervised loss than the baseline. They read this as evidence that the gain is not just faster convergence. — [ar5iv 1911.01218](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01218)
- Semi-supervised segmentation (background): one-way consistency, with the stop-gradient on the clean branch, beat two-way consistency in Grubišić et al. — [arXiv 2106.07075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.07075)

### Inferences
- The general claim "consistency adds something beyond supervising several views" has precedent: Botev et al. in classification with matched views, FACT and SHADE with CE on both views, and GJS. FCT's finding that its views alone do not beat vanilla while views plus both pulls do is consistent with these. It is new in three respects: segmentation, a deterministic flip orbit, and three host networks with unseen-centre tests.
- FCT's result that single pulls do not beat vanilla is at odds with two prior findings: FACT's single-direction gains, and the semi-supervised finding that one-way is better than two-way. It is a distinctive empirical claim worth stating carefully. It is also a likely target for reviewer questions about seed variance, because these single-direction effects are small (FACT's single-direction gains are 0.2 to 0.4 points).
- Because multi-sample augmentation alone can help (Fort, Hoffer), FCT's compute-matched "views only" row is a necessary control. SCT is a polyp precedent that lacked this control.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that ablates deterministic flip views (all views every step) against random single-view flips and against views plus consistency in any segmentation task.
- Botev et al. appears to be an arXiv preprint only: Crossref returned no peer-reviewed version and none was found elsewhere.
- None of the separating ablations above (FACT, SHADE, Botev, GJS) reports seed variance in the fetched text, so the significance of small differences cannot be judged from these sources.

## 4. Which works would a MICCAI reviewer most likely cite against FCT's novelty, and what is the strongest honest distinction?

### Takeaway
The most damaging prior work is SCT (IEEE ISM 2022). It has the same task, a similar train/test protocol (Kvasir-SEG training; ETIS and CVC-ClinicDB unseen), a fully supervised consistency between augmented and unaugmented views (including 90-degree rotations), a label-aware loss, and its largest gains on unseen data, with no in-distribution gain. A reviewer would pair it with:
- AugMix and R-Drop (generic supervised consistency);
- Bortsova et al. (MICCAI 2019; supervised-only transformation consistency in segmentation);
- FACT (two directed pulls; both beat one);
- KDCL (the label chooses the best view as the target).

Given these, FCT's core as currently framed (views plus two pulls) is largely anticipated. The strongest honest distinction is narrower: per pixel, within one network, the label decides which view teaches which. The upright view teaches a flipped view unless that flipped view is more accurate; then the more accurate flipped view teaches the upright view; and nothing is pulled toward an upright prediction that is wrong. Add to that the evidence, across three host networks, that both pulls are needed and that views alone do not help.

### Cited Findings
- SCT setting and results: polyps, Kvasir-SEG training, OOD tests on Etis-LaribDB, CVC-ClinicDB and EndoCV2020, label-aware SIL. Gains are largest on Etis (+0.024 to +0.035 mIoU), while Kvasir-SEG in-distribution changes by at most 0.004. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.15428); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ism55400.2022.00012)
- Bortsova et al. (MICCAI) report a supervised-only consistency variant (SupTC) for segmentation. — [ar5iv 1911.01218](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01218)
- FACT's dual-direction co-teacher ablation (a2o, o2a, both). — [ar5iv 2105.11120](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.11120)
- KDCL-Naive's ground-truth-selected target, "choose the logit with the smallest cross-entropy loss among all students" (Eq. 4), with students fed differently distorted images. — [CVF PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)
- GJS puts the label inside the multi-view JS target. — [ar5iv 2105.04522](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.04522)
- AugMix's JSD consistency, with its corruption-robustness gains. — [ar5iv 1912.02781](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.02781)
- R-Drop's bidirectional KL. — [arXiv 2106.14448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14448)
- Consisaug, a polyp-specific flip-consistency method under full supervision (detection, MLMI 2023). — [ar5iv 2404.11355](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2404.11355)
- SHADE and Ouyang et al. both use CE on two views plus a consistency term for DG segmentation, natural and medical respectively. — [ar5iv 2204.02548](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2204.02548); [ar5iv 2111.12525](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2111.12525)
- Equivariance under flips as a supervised segmentation loss (NeurIPS 2022). — [ar5iv 2210.00911](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.00911)
- Self-distillation of a multi-view aggregated inference into a single inference under full supervision (SLA, ICML 2020). This anticipates "upright-only test after multi-view training". — [arXiv 1910.05872](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.05872)
- The draft's related work (/home/user/BACFR/paper/sections/related.tex) does not cite SCT, FACT, KDCL, GJS, Botev et al., SHADE, Ouyang et al., Consisaug or the NeurIPS 2022 equivariance work. It presents Bortsova et al. only as semi-supervised. It also states: "All these methods treat the views as equally reliable or weigh them by the model's own confidence, since none uses the label to compare views". KDCL-Naive selects among differently distorted views/peers by their CE to the label, and GJS and SCT also use the label inside view-consistency objectives (sources as above).

### Inferences
- Likely reviewer citations, ranked, with the honest distinction for each:
  1. **SCT (ISM 2022).** "Consistency training for OOD polyp segmentation already exists." Distinction: SCT uses one random augmented view, a symmetric set-based loss in the image frame without mapping back, and a batch-level IoU weight between segmentation and consistency. It does not control for the number of views (stated as a limitation) and reports no ablation on direction. FCT supervises three deterministic flip views, maps them back, and uses directed, detached, per-pixel label-gated pulls, with a compute-matched views-only control across three modern hosts. FCT should cite SCT and, ideally, compare to SIL as a baseline consistency loss.
  2. **AugMix / R-Drop / DDGSD.** "Supervised consistency between views is known." Distinction: these are symmetric and label-agnostic. FCT's novelty cannot rest on adding a consistency term.
  3. **Bortsova et al. (MICCAI 2019), alongside the already-cited TCSM.** "Transformation/equivariance consistency for segmentation is known, including on labeled data." Distinction for Bortsova SupTC: gradients flow through both branches, there is no label awareness, and evaluation is in-distribution. Bortsova explicitly argues against fixed targets, which FCT uses. TCSM is a semi-supervised consistency method on unlabeled data; its internals were not re-checked here.
  4. **FACT (CVPR 2021).** "Two-way consistency between the original and augmented views, with ablations of each direction, is known." Distinction: FACT's two pulls are both always on and target an EMA teacher regardless of correctness. FCT's pulls are gated per pixel by the label, so their direction depends on which view is more accurate. Also, in FCT single pulls do not beat vanilla, whereas in FACT they do.
  5. **KDCL (CVPR 2020), with CA-MKD (already cited).** "Using the label to choose the best view or peer as the target is known." Distinction: KDCL works at sample level, across separate networks with random distortions, in classification, and pulls all students to one target. FCT works per pixel, within one network, on deterministic flips, and keeps the upright (test-time) view privileged: it is the default teacher and is overridden only where a flipped view is more accurate. FCT's margin gate also zeroes the pull where the upright prediction is wrong.
  6. **GJS (NeurIPS 2021).** "The label is already part of multi-view consistency targets." Distinction: in GJS the label is a fixed-weight component of a symmetric divergence and is not used to compare views.
  7. **SHADE / Ouyang et al. / equivariant segmentation / Consisaug.** Supporting examples of supervised two-view consistency in segmentation, DG and polyp detection.
- What should not be claimed as new: multi-view supervision with flipped labels; a cross-view consistency term under full supervision; mapping flipped predictions back (equivariance); a stop-gradient pull toward the clean or upright view; having two directions of pull; using the label in a consistency target; OOD or unseen-centre gains from supervised consistency (SCT shows the same pattern on polyps).
- What appears new (not found in any source checked):
  - (i) a per-pixel, label-decided direction of consistency between views of one image within one network;
  - (ii) the asymmetric treatment of the test-time view (the default teacher, demoted only where a flipped view is closer to the label, and never a target where it is wrong);
  - (iii) the systematic dissection showing that, for flips in polyp segmentation, both pulls are needed and views alone or single pulls do not beat vanilla, across Polyp-PVT, PraNet-V2 and SAM2-UNet.
- Framing risk: the paper's story ("multi-view consistency, views plus both pulls, is the core; the label-aware weights complete it") leads with the least novel part. If the unweighted both-pull variant already captures most of the gain, the method's methodological novelty rests on a small increment from label gating, and a reviewer can call the core "SCT/AugMix/FACT applied to flips". A more defensible framing:
  - Lead with the label-decided direction, "the more accurate view teaches", as the method.
  - Present "views plus both pulls" as the empirical finding: symmetric agreement is necessary and views alone are not enough, with SCT, FACT and Botev et al. cited as consistent prior evidence.
  - Report how much the label gating adds over the unweighted both-pull variant, with seed variance.
- Suggested related-work fixes:
  - Add SCT, FACT, KDCL, GJS and Botev et al.
  - Describe Bortsova's supervised-only SupTC.
  - Rewrite "none uses the label to compare views" as: prior supervised consistency treats views symmetrically or with a fixed direction; where the label enters, it is an extra target (GJS), a reference for the expected change (SCT), or a sample-level selector of an ensemble target across peer networks (KDCL); none decides per pixel which view teaches which by comparing each view's error with the label.
  - Consider citing SLA for distilling a multi-view prediction into single-view inference.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for a per-pixel, label-gated, direction-switching consistency between transformed views in any fully supervised segmentation work. This is absence of evidence from a non-exhaustive search (web, arXiv, Crossref and proceedings indexes; Google Scholar not queried directly), not proof of absence.
- Whether SCT's code applies the segmentation loss to the augmented view as well (the paper's equations put it on the unaugmented pair only) was not checked.
