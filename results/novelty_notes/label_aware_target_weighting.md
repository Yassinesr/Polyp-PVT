# Ground-truth-aware weighting and selection of teacher targets (KD, mutual/online learning, self-distillation), as it bears on the novelty of FCT's lag-or-margin weight and best-view gate

Scope note. FCT, as checked here: per pixel, after mapping flipped predictions back to the upright frame, with errors e_u = |p_u - y| and e_f = |p_f - y|:
(a) each flipped view f is pulled toward sg(p_u) with w = 1 if e_f >= e_u, else max(0, 1 - a*e_u), a = 2;
(b) the upright view is pulled toward sg(p_b), b = argmin_f e_f, only where e_b < e_u.
Already cited in the draft (`paper/sections/related.tex`): knowledge adjustment (Wen 2021), refined logits (Sun, ICCV 2025), label revision (Lan, TCDS 2025), CA-MKD (ICASSP 2022), structured KD for segmentation (Liu, CVPR 2019), DDGSD (AAAI 2019), BAN, Tang 2020, PS-KD, BYOT, Tf-KD/label smoothing (Yuan, CVPR 2020), DKD, Jeong (ICLR 2025), and the mean-teacher / FixMatch line. Everything below is not in the draft unless marked "(already cited)". Each paper's formulation was checked against its full text (PDF) unless noted otherwise.

## Q1. Which KD / self-distillation methods mask or down-weight teacher targets where the teacher is wrong with respect to the label, or weight by teacher-versus-student error (incl. segmentation and medical segmentation)?

### Takeaway
This is an established family, and segmentation already has it **per pixel**. One segmentation-KD weight is zero wherever the student is at least as accurate as the teacher on the true class (Shan 2019). Another swaps in the one-hot label wherever the teacher's pixel is wrong (Park & Heo, Sensors 2020). Medical-segmentation KD corrects the teacher's false pixels (PLC-KD, MICCAI 2024). Outside segmentation, the student-versus-teacher error comparison appears as a hard gate (teacher-bounded loss, NIPS 2017), as a smooth weight (weighted soft labels, ICLR 2021), and as a weight that falls with the teacher's error (attentive imitation loss, ICCV 2019; IAD, ICLR 2022). So neither "zero the pull where the target is wrong" nor "gate by which of the two is closer to the label" is new to FCT.

### Cited Findings
**Segmentation-specific, per pixel**
- Shan, "Distilling Pixel-Wise Feature Similarities for Semantic Segmentation" (arXiv 1910.14226, single author Yuhu Shan; I found no peer-reviewed venue). It is offline KD from a fixed, pre-trained teacher. Per pixel n, a soft cross-entropy pulls the student toward the teacher, H_soft^n = -sum_i P_t,n^i log P_s,n^i, weighted by mu*w_n with **w_n = max(0, p*_t,n - p*_s,n)**, where p* is each network's softmax probability on the ground-truth class. The weight is "set to 0 to avoid the knowledge transfer from the teacher to student in that pixel" when the teacher is less confident than the student on the true class. A hard-label CE and an L1 pixel-similarity loss are added alongside — [arXiv/ar5iv 1910.14226](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.14226)
- Park & Heo, "Knowledge Distillation for Semantic Segmentation Using Channel and Spatial Correlations and Adaptive Cross Entropy", *Sensors* 20(16):4616, 2020 (DOI 10.3390/s20164616). The adaptive cross-entropy (ACE) target per pixel is P(i,j) = kappa*p_t(i,j) + (1-kappa)*p_g(i,j) if the teacher's predicted label L_t(i,j) equals the ground truth G(i,j), and p_g(i,j) (one-hot ground truth) otherwise, "to block transferring error of the teacher network" — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/16/4616); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7471971/)
- Liu et al., structured KD for segmentation (pixel-wise, pair-wise and holistic distillation) uses no label-aware gating of the teacher (already cited) — [CVPR 2019 paper via draft bib, DOI 10.1109/CVPR.2019.00271](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR.2019.00271)

**Medical image segmentation**
- Wang, Cao, Hou, Lan, Yang, Liu, Zaiane, "Progressively Correcting Soft Labels via Teacher Team for Knowledge Distillation in Medical Image Segmentation" (PLC-KD), MICCAI 2024. The paper argues that correcting the teacher's false predictions "to fixed correct values" is sub-optimal. A teacher team (the teacher, a false-pixel-aware assistant and a boundary-aware assistant) progressively corrects false soft labels, and a similarity-based correction "forcefully rectif[ies] the remaining hard false pixels", so that the student is guided by "all-correct" soft labels — [MICCAI 2024 paper PDF](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2024/paper/2751_paper.pdf)
- Wu, Zhang, Piao, Yao, Sun, Tian, Lu, "CNN-Transformer Rectified Collaborative Learning for Medical Image Segmentation" (CTRCL), IEEE TCSVT, issue dated May 2025, DOI 10.1109/TCSVT.2024.3523316. It is a mutual (two-student) method; details are under Q2. Its adaptive rectification module finds each student's wrong pixels with M = XOR(argmax(P), Y) and rectifies those soft-label pixels with the ground truth before the mutual KL. It is **evaluated on Kvasir-SEG** (polyp), Synapse and ACDC — [arXiv 2408.13698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13698); [Crossref DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCSVT.2024.3523316)

**Sample-level (classification, speech, regression, detection)**
- Zhou et al., "Rethinking Soft Labels for Knowledge Distillation: A Bias-Variance Tradeoff Perspective" (weighted soft labels, WSL), ICLR 2021. It weights each sample's KD loss by **L_wsl = (1 - exp(-L_ce^s / L_ce^t)) * L_kd**, where L_ce^s and L_ce^t are the student's and teacher's CE on the ground-truth class. When the student is relatively better trained than the teacher on a sample, the sample gets a smaller weight — [arXiv 2102.00650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00650); [ICLR 2021 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2021/poster/2807)
- WSL also reports that a hard gate is worse than plain KD. It names "regularization samples" those where the variance term of the gradient dominates the bias term, and proxies them at tau = 1 by comparing student and teacher probabilities on the GT class. In its Table 2 (CIFAR-100, tau = 4, 5 runs), the teacher scores 75.61, direct training 73.26 and standard KD 74.92. KD only on non-regularization samples (a hard "lag-only" style gate) gives 74.59, and KD only on regularization samples gives 73.86. "Completely filtering out regularization samples also deteriorates distillation performance" — [arXiv 2102.00650 (PDF)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.00650)
- Chen, Choi, Yu, Han, Chandraker, "Learning Efficient Object Detection Models with Knowledge Distillation", NIPS 2017. Its **teacher bounded regression loss** is L_b(R_s, R_t, y) = ||R_s - y||^2 if ||R_s - y||^2 + m > ||R_t - y||^2, and 0 otherwise, with L_reg = L_sL1(R_s, y) + nu*L_b and nu = 0.5. It "only penalizes the network when the error of the student is larger than that of the teacher". Note that the pull is toward the **ground truth**, with the teacher's error acting as a gate or upper bound, not toward the teacher's output — [NIPS 2017 paper PDF](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/6676-learning-efficient-object-detection-models-with-knowledge-distillation.pdf)
- Saputra et al., "Distilling Knowledge From a Deep Pose Regressor Network", ICCV 2019. Its **attentive imitation loss** is L_reg = (1/n) sum_i [alpha*||p_S - p_gt||^2 + (1 - alpha)*Phi_i*||p_S - p_T||^2], with **Phi_i = 1 - ||p_T - p_gt||^2 / eta** and eta = max(e_T) - min(e_T) over the teacher's errors on the whole training set. The imitation weight falls as the teacher's error grows, and the same Phi_i weights hint training (AHT). It does not compare against the student's error — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Saputra_Distilling_Knowledge_From_a_Deep_Pose_Regressor_Network_ICCV_2019_paper.html); [arXiv 1908.00858](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00858)
- Zhu et al., "Reliable Adversarial Distillation with Unreliable Teachers" (introspective adversarial distillation, IAD), ICLR 2022. The teacher's term is weighted by **alpha = (P_T(x~|y))^beta**, the teacher's probability of the true label on the adversarial input, with beta = 0.1. The student "partially instead of fully trust[s]" the teacher and adds a self-introspection term gamma*KL(S(x~|tau) || S(x|tau)) between its own adversarial and natural views. The abstract states three cases: fully trust a teacher that is good on the adversarial data, partially trust one that is good only on natural data, otherwise rely on the student itself — [arXiv 2106.04928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04928); [ICLR 2022 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6336)
- Meng, Li, Zhao, Gong, "Conditional Teacher-Student Learning", ICASSP 2019 (speech). The student "selectively chooses to learn from either the teacher model or the ground truth labels conditioned on whether the teacher can correctly predict the ground truth" — [arXiv 1904.12399](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12399); [DOI 10.1109/ICASSP.2019.8683438](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICASSP.2019.8683438)
- Yang, Ochal, Storkey, Crowley, "Prediction-Guided Distillation for Dense Object Detection" (PGD), ECCV 2022. It computes a quality score of each teacher prediction with respect to the ground-truth box and distills features only at the top-K "key predictive regions" of the teacher, which is a label-aware choice of *where* to distill — [arXiv 2203.05469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05469); [DOI 10.1007/978-3-031-20077-9_8](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-031-20077-9_8)
- Already cited in the draft: knowledge adjustment (corrects teacher predictions that contradict the label), refined logits, label revision, and CA-MKD (weights teachers by closeness to the label) — see `paper/sections/related.tex`, lines 87-91

### Inferences
- With a = 2, FCT's margin branch max(0, 1 - 2*e_u) is zero exactly where e_u >= 0.5, that is, where the upright pixel thresholded at 0.5 is wrong. Its zero set is therefore the per-pixel teacher-correctness mask of ACE (Park & Heo 2020) and Conditional T/S, with an AIL-style linear ramp (Saputra 2019) between 0 and 0.5.
- FCT's lag condition (full weight where e_f >= e_u) is the teacher-bounded condition of Chen 2017 with m = 0, and the complement of Shan's zero set. Shan's weight is max(0, p*_t - p*_s): zero where the student leads, and proportional to the teacher's lead where the student lags. FCT instead gives w = 1 where the student lags and a non-zero weight in part of the lead region, so the shapes differ.
- WSL is the strongest precedent for the margin idea: keep a reduced, non-zero pull where the student already beats the teacher. It also reports evidence (74.59 vs 74.92) that a hard lag-only gate underperforms full KD in classification. FCT's own result that a lag-only gate also works is consistent with WSL's finding that the gate's exact form matters little relative to having KD at all.
- None of the Q1 methods operates between transformed views of one image within one network. All are teacher-to-student between two networks, except IAD's self-introspection term, which is between views but is not label-gated per view comparison.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed venue for Shan (arXiv 1910.14226). A reviewer may still know it, but it is weaker to cite than the CVPR/ICCV/MICCAI items.
- Search snippets pointed to further medical-segmentation KD/self-KD with reliability weighting: "Uncertainty Driven Adaptive Self-Knowledge Distillation for Medical Image Segmentation" (IEEE, doc 10857643), "Feature distance-weighted adaptive decoupled KD for medical image segmentation" (IJCARS 2025), and "Enhancing learning on uncertain pixels in self-distillation for object segmentation" (Complex & Intelligent Systems 2024, which fuses predictions with GT). I did not read them, and the snippets suggest uncertainty-based rather than label-comparison weights, so they are unverified here.
- I did not exhaustively survey LLM/NLP selective distillation (e.g., token-level teacher-correctness filtering), which is off-domain for a MICCAI reviewer.

## Q2. Which online / mutual / collaborative / peer methods pick, per sample or per pixel, the peer closest to the label as the target, or make the pull direction depend on which side is more accurate?

### Takeaway
The closest prior work to FCT's best-view gate exists, per pixel, in segmentation. Zhu et al. (ICCV 2023) decide the direction of a KL pull between a CNN and a ViT student **per pixel by which of the two has the lower cross-entropy with the ground truth**. This is the same criterion as FCT's gate for binary labels, applied between two networks on the same input instead of between views of one network. KDCL (CVPR 2020) picks, per sample, the student with the minimum CE against the label as the teacher, and each student receives its own randomly distorted copy of the input. CTRCL (IEEE TCSVT 2025, evaluated on Kvasir-SEG) uses the ground truth to rectify each peer's wrong pixels before mutual learning. SwitOKD (ECCV 2022) switches between one-way and two-way distillation with a label-referenced threshold.

### Cited Findings
- Zhu, Luo, Zheng, Wang, Wang, "A Good Student is Cooperative and Reliable: CNN-Transformer Collaborative Learning for Semantic Segmentation", ICCV 2023, pp. 11720-11730 (DOI 10.1109/ICCV51070.2023.01076). It is online KD between a CNN student (DeepLabV3+) and a ViT student (SegFormer), fully supervised, on PASCAL VOC 2012, Cityscapes and CamVid. In **pixel-wise bidirectional selective distillation (BSD)**, a binary map m(h,w) = 1 "when the CE of pixel from CNN-based student is smaller than that of ViT", which transfers knowledge CNN to ViT, "and vice versa for m(h,w) = 0". The losses are L_P^C = 1/(HW - M) * sum (1 - m)*KL(P^C || P^V) and L_P^V = (1/M) * sum m*KL(P^V || P^C), where M = sum m. **Region-wise BSD** applies the same CE-versus-GT rule per feature region to weight a cross-student cosine similarity. The paper states that both "are both guided by the cross entropy between predictions and ground-truth (GT) labels". The total loss is L = L_CE + 0.1*L_HFD + 1*L_BSD. Whether targets are detached is not stated in the text I read — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Zhu_A_Good_Student_is_Cooperative_and_Reliable_CNN-Transformer_Collaborative_Learning_ICCV_2023_paper.html); [arXiv 2307.12574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12574)
- Guo et al., "Online Knowledge Distillation via Collaborative Learning" (KDCL), CVPR 2020 (DOI 10.1109/CVPR42600.2020.01103). In **KDCL-Naive**, "A naive combination method is to choose the logit with the smallest cross-entropy loss among all students": z_t = z_k, k = argmin_i L_CE(z_i, y). The other variants also use the label. KDCL-Linear takes the best linear combination of student logits. KDCL-MinLogit subtracts the true-class logit and takes the per-class minimum. KDCL-General fits ensemble weights on a held-out subset of the training set, updated each epoch. Inputs are "distorted separately for each network". The paper notes that "KDCL-Linear outperforms KDCL-Naive as a result of the higher quality soft target". The task is classification (CIFAR, ImageNet) — [CVPR 2020 paper PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR42600.2020.01103)
- Wu et al., CTRCL (IEEE TCSVT 2025). Its rectified logit-wise collaborative learning "introduces the ground truth to adaptively select and rectify the wrong regions in student soft labels". The error mask is M = XOR(argmax(P), Y). On those pixels, the soft label is mixed with the one-hot label using lambda = lambda_a*lambda_s*lambda_c, where lambda_a = (1 + p_mis - p_tru)^(-1), lambda_s = exp(-CE(P^m, Y^m)) and lambda_c = exp(-S(P^m)). The rectified soft labels of the CNN and Transformer students are then exchanged with KL. Datasets are Synapse, ACDC and Kvasir-SEG, and KDCL is among its baselines — [arXiv 2408.13698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13698); [DOI 10.1109/TCSVT.2024.3523316](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCSVT.2024.3523316)
- Qian, Wang, Yin, Hong, Wang, "Switchable Online Knowledge Distillation" (SwitOKD), ECCV 2022. The gap is G = ||p_s^tau - p_t^tau||_1, and the adaptive switching threshold is **delta = ||p_s^tau - y||_1 - exp(-||p_t^tau - y||_1 / (||p_s^tau - y||_1 + ||p_t^tau - y||_1)) * ||p_t^tau - y||_1** (Eq. 13). If G <= delta, the method uses "learning mode", in which teacher and student learn from each other. Otherwise it uses "expert mode", which freezes the teacher while the student learns. The direction (two-way or one-way) is therefore switched with label-referenced errors, computed each training iteration in Algorithm 1 — [arXiv 2209.04996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04996); [ECCV 2022 page](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/5410_ECCV_2022_paper.php)
- Ke, Wang, Yan, Ren, Lau, "Dual Student: Breaking the Limits of the Teacher in Semi-supervised Learning", ICCV 2019. This is the unlabeled analogue. It proposes to "exchange only reliable knowledge of the models" between two students via a stabilization constraint defined on "stable samples", that is, predictions that are unaffected by small perturbations, with stability graded by prediction consistency in the neighbourhood. The ground truth is not used to choose the direction — [arXiv 1909.01804](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01804); [DOI 10.1109/ICCV.2019.00683](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2019.00683)
- Zhu et al. (ICCV 2023) describe KDCL as aggregating students' outputs "to generate high-quality labels for supervision", and PCL as integrating "online ensemble and collaborative learning into a unified framework". They contrast both with their own label-guided bidirectional selection — [arXiv 2307.12574 (PDF, related work)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.12574)

### Inferences
- For binary masks, BCE(p, y) = -log(1 - |p - y|), which increases monotonically with |p - y|. Zhu's "lower CE versus GT" rule is therefore **identical** to FCT's "closer to the label" rule (e_b < e_u). FCT's pull (b), where the upright is pulled toward the best flipped view only where that view is more accurate, is Zhu's pixel-wise BSD rule transplanted from two networks to views of one network, with a third candidate and an argmin.
- KDCL-Naive is the per-sample, classification version of "the peer closest to the label is the target", and it already combines that rule with **different input distortions per peer**. The multi-view plus label-chosen-target combination is therefore not new at the sample level. KDCL's own finding that a combined target beats the single best peer suggests FCT could be challenged on why it takes the single best flipped view rather than a label-weighted combination.
- FCT differs from all of these by being asymmetric with an anchor. The upright view, the one used at test time, is privileged. The loser-to-winner pull is full, but the winner-to-loser pull is partial, and only for the flipped views toward a still-correct upright, via the margin branch. In Zhu's BSD and KDCL the winner gets no pull at all, and the peers' roles are symmetric.
- I found no online or mutual method that uses the label to arbitrate per pixel between predictions of the **same network on geometrically transformed inputs** (flips, rotations) mapped back to a common frame. This is FCT's distinctive setting.

### Gaps
- DML (CVPR 2018), ONE (NeurIPS 2018), OKDDip (AAAI 2020) and PCL (AAAI 2021) are, to my knowledge, not label-gated in direction: symmetric KL, a learned gate, attention weights and a peer-ensemble mean teacher respectively. I did not re-read their full texts in this pass. Only PCL's characterisation is sourced here, and only second-hand through Zhu 2023.
- Search snippets mentioned polyp mutual-learning methods: GAML (BSPC 2025), PedSemiSeg (2025, "Cross Reliable Knowledge Distillation"), S2ME (MICCAI 2023, scribble-supervised) and a CVPR 2025 depth-guided mutual-learning method. The snippets indicate uncertainty or reliability selection without GT comparison, mostly semi- or weakly-supervised. I did not verify them from full texts.
- I could not confirm from Zhu 2023's text whether its KL targets are stop-gradient.

## Q3. Which self-distillation methods across augmented views of the same image select or weight targets with the label?

### Takeaway
A few exist, all at the **sample level** and in classification or adversarial robustness. MDCS (ICCV 2023) distills weak-view predictions into strong views only for samples whose weak view is correctly classified. MART (ICLR 2020) weights the clean-to-adversarial KL by (1 - p_y(clean)). IAD (ICLR 2022) weights the teacher by its true-label probability and adds a natural-to-adversarial self-consistency term. KDCL feeds each peer a different distortion. I found none that compares the label-errors of two views of one image **per pixel** to set the weight or direction, and none for flip or rotation views in segmentation.

### Cited Findings
- Zhao, Jiang, Hu, Zhang, Liu, "MDCS: More Diverse Experts with Consistency Self-distillation for Long-tailed Recognition", ICCV 2023 (DOI 10.1109/ICCV51070.2023.01065). Consistency self-distillation goes from weakly augmented views to strongly augmented views. **Confident Instance Sampling** keeps only D_CI = {x_i : argmax p(x_bar_i; theta) = y_i}, where x_bar is the weak view, "to avoid biased/noisy knowledge". The label gates whether the cross-view pull is applied, but the two views' errors are not compared — [arXiv 2308.09922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09922); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV51070.2023.01065)
- Wang, Zou, Yi, Bailey, Ma, Gu, "Improving Adversarial Robustness Requires Revisiting Misclassified Examples" (MART), ICLR 2020. The objective is BCE(p(x_hat), y) + lambda*KL(p(x) || p(x_hat))*(1 - p_y(x)): a clean/adversarial view consistency weighted by the clean view's error on the true class, which **up-weights** consistency where the clean view is wrong, the opposite of FCT's margin branch. The same paper's comparison table lists MMA training as CE(p(x_hat), y)*1(h(x) = y) + CE(p(x), y)*1(h(x) != y), which uses correctness to pick which view to supervise — [ICLR 2020 poster page](https://iclr.cc/virtual_2020/poster_rklOg6EFwS.html); [paper text (ICLR 2020 corpus)](https://object.cloud.sdsc.edu/v1/AUTH_da4962d3368042ac8337e2dfdd3e7bf3/ml-papers-txt/ICLR/2020/improving_adversarial_robustness_requires_revisiting_misclassified_examples__12445a95.txt)
- IAD (ICLR 2022). Its loss is the adversarial-distillation baseline weighted by alpha = P_T(x~|y)^beta, plus gamma*KL(S(x~|tau) || S(x|tau)), which pulls the student's adversarial-view prediction toward its own natural-view prediction ("student introspection"). The label weights the teacher term, not the cross-view term — [arXiv 2106.04928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04928)
- KDCL (CVPR 2020) gives each network its own random distortion of the input and chooses the target with the label (KDCL-Naive: argmin CE), so this cross-view selection works through different networks — [CVPR 2020 paper PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)
- Data-distortion guided self-distillation makes one network agree with itself on two distorted copies under full supervision and treats both copies alike (already cited in the draft) — [DOI 10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33015565](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33015565)
- A search for flip or rotation consistency in segmentation with GT-gated or view-weighted terms returned only label-agnostic flip/rotation consistency in weakly supervised settings, e.g., Laradji et al., "A Weakly Supervised Consistency-based Learning Method for COVID-19 Segmentation in CT Images" (WACV 2021) — [CVF open access PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Laradji_A_Weakly_Supervised_Consistency-Based_Learning_Method_for_COVID-19_Segmentation_in_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The draft's sentence "All these methods treat the views as equally reliable or weigh them by the model's own confidence, since none uses the label to compare views" (`related.tex`, lines 62-64) is defensible only in a narrow form. MDCS and MART **do** use the label to gate or weight a cross-view consistency term, though on one view's correctness, not a comparison. KDCL compares label-errors of predictions on differently distorted inputs, though from different networks and per sample. A safer claim: no prior method compares, per pixel, the label-errors of one network's predictions on geometrically transformed views mapped back to a common frame in order to set the weight and direction of the consistency pull.
- The label-gated cross-view self-distillation that exists (MDCS, MART) uses one view's correctness. FCT's comparative criterion (which view is closer to y) among equivariant views of one network appears to be new as a combination. Its pieces have direct precedents: Zhu 2023 for the per-pixel comparison, KDCL for multiple distorted inputs with a label-chosen target, and MDCS for label-gated cross-view self-distillation.

### Gaps
- Search snippets surfaced "Glitter" (arXiv 2203.09391), which selects augmented samples by their loss against the label (worst-case selection, like MaxUp), and several 2026 arXiv self-distillation preprints (AVSD, VICAL). I did not verify their venues or formulations, so they are not used.
- Multi-view and flip-consistency work in general (unlabeled or semi-supervised) is out of this note's scope; the related-work file covers it (Bortsova 2019, TCSM, etc.).

## Q4. Is a "lag-or-margin" style weight (full weight when the student lags the teacher, falling to zero as the teacher's error grows when the student leads) known under another name?

### Takeaway
The exact piecewise form, w = 1 if e_s >= e_t, else max(0, 1 - a*e_t), did not turn up under any name. Its two halves are well-known ideas with names. The **lag gate** is the "teacher bounded" loss (Chen et al., NIPS 2017), "pixel-wise selective / bidirectional selective distillation" (Zhu et al., ICCV 2023), Shan's knowledge-gap weight max(0, p*_t - p*_s), and the per-pixel hint rule of Depth Hints (ICCV 2019). The **margin ramp** that falls with the teacher's error is the "attentive imitation loss" (Saputra et al., ICCV 2019) and IAD's "partially trust" alpha = P_T(y)^beta. Teacher-correctness masks such as ACE and Conditional T/S are its hard limit. The closest single smooth relative is **weighted soft labels** (ICLR 2021): w = 1 - exp(-L_s/L_t), near 1 when the student lags and decaying toward 0 as the student's lead grows.

### Cited Findings
- Teacher-bounded regression loss: the student is penalized only when ||R_s - y||^2 + m > ||R_t - y||^2, and "does not push the student too much once it reaches the teacher's performance" — [Chen et al., NIPS 2017](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/6676-learning-efficient-object-detection-models-with-knowledge-distillation.pdf)
- Attentive imitation loss: the per-sample imitation weight is Phi_i = 1 - ||p_T - p_gt||^2/eta, a linear-in-squared-error ramp in the teacher's own error, normalized by the range of teacher errors on the training set — [Saputra et al., ICCV 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Saputra_Distilling_Knowledge_From_a_Deep_Pose_Regressor_Network_ICCV_2019_paper.html)
- Weighted soft labels: w = 1 - exp(-L_ce^s/L_ce^t), where "a student network is relatively better trained on a sample" gets a smaller weight — [Zhou et al., ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00650)
- Knowledge-gap pixel weight w_n = max(0, p*_t,n - p*_s,n) in segmentation KD — [Shan, arXiv 1910.14226](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.14226)
- Pixel-wise BSD mask m(h,w) set by which network has the lower CE with GT — [Zhu et al., ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Zhu_A_Good_Student_is_Cooperative_and_Reliable_CNN-Transformer_Collaborative_Learning_ICCV_2023_paper.html)
- Watson, Firman, Brostow, Turmukhambetov, "Self-Supervised Monocular Depth Hints", ICCV 2019 (DOI 10.1109/ICCV.2019.00225). Per pixel, l_ours(d_i) = l_r(d_i) + l_slogL1(d_i, h_i) if l_r(h_i) < l_r(d_i), and l_r(d_i) otherwise. The network is pulled toward an alternative hypothesis "only when the reprojection loss can be improved upon". This is the same "pull toward the other prediction only where it is more accurate" gate, with the photometric loss in place of the label — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/html/Watson_Self-Supervised_Monocular_Depth_Hints_ICCV_2019_paper.html)
- IAD's "partially instead of fully trust" weight alpha = (P_T(x~|y))^beta — [Zhu et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04928)
- Teacher-correctness switches to the label: ACE in segmentation KD ([Park & Heo, Sensors 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/16/4616)) and Conditional Teacher-Student Learning in speech ([Meng et al., ICASSP 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12399))
- SwitOKD's label-referenced threshold delta = e_s - exp(-e_t/(e_s + e_t))*e_t (with e = ||p - y||_1) mixes the student's and teacher's errors to decide whether distillation is one-way or two-way — [Qian et al., ECCV 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04996)

### Inferences
- FCT's lag-or-margin weight is the teacher-bounded / selective-distillation gate (Chen 2017 with m = 0; Zhu 2023; Shan 2019), with the zero region replaced by an AIL-style ramp in the teacher's error (Saputra 2019). Its zero point at a = 2 is the teacher-correctness mask (ACE / Conditional T/S). Fair names for it in the paper are "a teacher-bounded consistency weight with a correctness-aware margin" or "a soft teacher-bounded weight". Claiming it as a new principle would be hard to defend. Claiming the specific form, and its use between views, is defensible.
- Functionally, WSL's weight depends on the **ratio** of student to teacher error, while FCT's margin branch depends only on the **teacher's absolute error** once the student leads. FCT therefore keeps pulling a better flipped view toward an upright that is correct but slightly less accurate, and stops only when the upright approaches being wrong. This is a real, if small, design difference to state explicitly.
- FCT's ablations already show that w = 1, the lag-only gate and a in {0.5, 1.5, 2} all work. Together with WSL's Table 2, this means the weight should be presented as a refinement that improves gradient alignment, not as the source of the gains.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that analyses such a gate by the cosine alignment between the consistency gradient and the supervised gradient. The closest analyses are WSL's per-sample decomposition of the KD gradient into bias (CE) and variance terms, and the KD-gradient rescaling analyses already cited (BAN, Tang 2020). I did not search the multi-task gradient-conflict literature (PCGrad and similar), which may also be raised.
- Possible further analogues in RL (e.g., self-imitation learning, which imitates past actions only when their return exceeds the current value estimate) were not verified in this pass and are not cited.

## Q5. Which of these would a MICCAI reviewer cite against FCT, and what is the strongest honest distinction? (Novelty verdict)

### Takeaway
The most damaging citations are **Zhu et al. ICCV 2023**, whose per-pixel GT-CE rule decides which prediction teaches which and is mathematically the same criterion as FCT's best-view gate for binary masks, and **KDCL (CVPR 2020)**, where the lowest-CE peer is the target and each peer gets a separately distorted input. A medical reviewer would add **CTRCL (IEEE TCSVT 2025)**, which rectifies wrong pixels with GT in mutual learning and is evaluated on Kvasir-SEG, and **PLC-KD (MICCAI 2024)**, which corrects a teacher's false pixels for medical segmentation KD. Shan 2019, ACE, WSL and teacher-bounded KD back up "label-aware teacher gating is known". The strongest honest distinction is the **setting and the asymmetric structure**: the rule is applied inside one network, between flip views of the same image whose outputs are compared pixel-for-pixel after inverse mapping, with an upright anchor view and no second model. It is not the gating principle itself. The weight's specific "lag-or-margin" form is a modest variation.

### Cited Findings
- Per-pixel label-arbitrated direction between two segmentation networks, fully supervised — [Zhu et al., ICCV 2023](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Zhu_A_Good_Student_is_Cooperative_and_Reliable_CNN-Transformer_Collaborative_Learning_ICCV_2023_paper.html)
- Per-sample min-CE peer as target, with separately distorted inputs per peer — [Guo et al., CVPR 2020](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)
- GT-rectified mutual learning for medical segmentation, including Kvasir-SEG — [Wu et al., IEEE TCSVT 2025 / arXiv 2408.13698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13698)
- Correcting teacher false pixels in medical segmentation KD — [Wang et al., MICCAI 2024](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2024/paper/2751_paper.pdf)
- Per-pixel teacher-vs-student GT-probability weight in segmentation KD — [Shan, arXiv 1910.14226](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1910.14226); per-pixel teacher-correctness target in segmentation KD — [Park & Heo, Sensors 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/16/4616)
- Smooth student/teacher-error weight, plus evidence that a hard lag-only gate underperforms KD — [Zhou et al., ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00650)

### Inferences
**Likely reviewer citations, ranked by threat**
1. Zhu et al. ICCV 2023: the same per-pixel "lower CE vs. GT teaches" rule, in fully supervised segmentation. This is the main threat to the best-view gate.
2. KDCL (CVPR 2020): "best peer by label = target" combined with per-peer input distortions, which pre-empts "label-chosen target across views" at the sample level.
3. CTRCL (TCSVT 2025): a polyp dataset plus GT-guided pixel selection in collaborative learning, likely to be known to MICCAI reviewers working on polyps.
4. PLC-KD (MICCAI 2024): a MICCAI-venue precedent for GT-aware pixel-level correction of distillation targets.
5. WSL (ICLR 2021) and teacher-bounded KD (NIPS 2017): precedents for the weight.
6. MDCS (ICCV 2023) and MART (ICLR 2020): precedents for label-aware cross-view consistency.
7. Already cited: KA, RLD, label revision, CA-MKD, DDGSD.

**Novelty judgement**
- *Best-view gate alone* has low novelty. It is Zhu 2023's pixel-wise BSD criterion (and KDCL-Naive's argmin at the sample level, and Depth Hints' per-pixel rule) applied to views.
- *Lag-or-margin weight alone* has low to moderate novelty. The principle is known (teacher-bounded, WSL, AIL, ACE/Conditional T/S). The exact piecewise form, full weight when lagging and a ramp in the target's absolute error when leading, was not found, but it is a small variation. FCT's own ablations show the gains do not depend on it.
- *Combined with flip views in a single network* has moderate novelty. I found no prior work that uses the label to set the per-pixel direction and weight of consistency between geometrically transformed views of one image within one network under full supervision. Neither did I find the specific asymmetric design (an upright anchor that is pulled only where a flipped view beats it, and flipped views pulled toward the anchor with a correctness-aware margin), nor this combination for polyp or medical segmentation. The closest relatives are KDCL (views plus label-chosen target, but different networks, per sample) and MDCS (label-gated cross-view self-distillation, but one-view correctness, per sample, classification).

**Strongest honest distinctions to write**
- (i) **No second model.** The "peers" are one network's predictions on flip views. The comparison is per pixel because the flip is an exact, invertible spatial map, so the views' errors are comparable at each pixel after mapping back. No peer network, pre-trained teacher, EMA model or extra parameters are needed, and inference is a single pass.
- (ii) **Asymmetric, test-view-anchored design.** Unlike the symmetric winner-teaches rules of Zhu 2023 and KDCL, the upright view used at test time is the anchor. It is pulled only toward a flipped view that beats it. Flipped views are pulled toward it with a margin that keeps a partial pull where the anchor is still correct, a choice in line with WSL's evidence that dropping all "student-leads" samples hurts.
- (iii) **Analysis.** FCT reports an identity in which the consistency term reweights the supervised loss, and shows that the label-aware weights roughly double the cosine alignment between the consistency gradient and the supervised gradient. Prior gates are justified heuristically ("reliable knowledge", "upper bound") or through WSL's bias-variance gradient decomposition. FCT should cite WSL as the nearest analysis.

**Suggested edits to the draft's related work**
- Cite Zhu 2023, KDCL, CTRCL, PLC-KD, WSL and teacher-bounded KD (plus Shan 2019 or ACE for pixel-wise segmentation KD), and MDCS/MART in the consistency paragraph.
- Replace "FCT ... neither corrects a target nor ranks separate teachers, but weights each pixel's consistency term by comparing the errors of two views with the label" (`related.tex`, lines 92-95). Per-pixel error comparison with the label is exactly what Zhu's BSD does, and the best-view gate does rank candidate targets with the label, as KDCL-Naive does. A safer contrast: "prior label-arbitrated distillation compares two networks (or a teacher and a student) on the same input; FCT compares one network's predictions on flip views of the same image, pixel by pixel, with the test-time view as anchor."
- Narrow "none uses the label to compare views" (`related.tex`, lines 62-64), as discussed under Q3.

### Gaps
- No exhaustive check of MICCAI 2025 / MedIA / TMI 2025-2026 for a flip-view, label-gated consistency method. Several phrasings (flip/rotation consistency + ground truth gating; self-distillation + augmented views + ground truth; mutual learning + pixel-wise + ground truth) returned nothing matching. This is evidence of absence only to the extent of those searches.
- Whether Zhu 2023 detaches its targets, and whether its gains come mainly from BSD or from HFD, was not extracted (HFD is its feature-alignment component). That would matter if a reviewer claims FCT's pull (b) is identical to BSD.
