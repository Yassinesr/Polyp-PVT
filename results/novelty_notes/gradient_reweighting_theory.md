# Consistency and distillation gradients as reweighted supervision: prior work on FCT's analytical claim (Prop. 1, Corollary 1, Eq. combined, cosine measurement)

Scope: whether the identity dl(z_s; p_t)/dz_s = p_s - p_t = (1 - e_t/e_s)(p_s - y) (binary label, detached target), its reading (consistency/distillation = per-example or per-pixel reweighting of supervision whose sign is set by which prediction is closer to the label), the combined form (beta + gamma*omega)(p_s - y), the label-aware gating and the gradient-cosine measurement already appear in prior work, and how the FCT paper (draft: `paper/sections/analysis.tex`, `method.tex`, `related.tex`) should credit them. All papers below were checked against a primary source (arXiv record or PDF, PMLR, CVF open access, OpenReview, NeurIPS proceedings, MICCAI proceedings). Equations quoted from PDFs were read in the downloaded full text.

## Q1. Where has the gradient of distillation or consistency been decomposed relative to the ground-truth gradient?

### Takeaway
The knowledge-distillation (KD) literature has done this decomposition several times. Furlanello et al. (ICML 2018) split the KD logit gradient into a true-class term, which they read as importance weighting of the ground-truth gradient, and a dark-knowledge term. Tang et al. (arXiv 2020) define the exact per-logit ratio of the KD gradient to the CE gradient as a "gradient rescaling factor". Kim et al. (PS-KD, ICCV 2021) write the resulting factor as `1 - alpha * (gamma_{t-1}/gamma_t)`, with gamma the probability of being incorrect. This is FCT's combined factor. Zhou et al. (ICLR 2021) analyse its sign on the label logit, and Zhao et al. (DKD, CVPR 2022) isolate the binary target-class part. Hinton et al. (2015), Yuan et al. (CVPR 2020), Mobahi et al. (NeurIPS 2020), Menon et al. (ICML 2021) and Phuong & Lampert (ICML 2019) do not give this decomposition. Yuan gives the equivalent loss-level "mixed target" form, and Menon and Mobahi cite the weighting reading of Furlanello and Tang.

### Cited Findings
- **Hinton et al. 2015 (arXiv:1503.02531).** Gives the soft-target gradient `dC/dz_i = (1/T)(q_i - p_i)` and its high-temperature limit, logit matching. When labels are available, it uses "a weighted average of two different objective functions" and multiplies the soft-target term by T^2, because the soft-target gradients scale as 1/T^2. The paper does not compare the soft-target gradient with the ground-truth gradient. — [Hinton et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531)
- **Furlanello et al., Born-Again Networks (ICML 2018, PMLR 80), Sec. 3.2 "Dark Knowledge Under the Light".**
  - The single-sample gradient is `dL_i/dz_i = q_i - p_i` (Eq. 5). It reduces to `q_* - 1` for a one-hot label (Eq. 6).
  - Over a minibatch, the KD gradient splits into a true-class term `sum_s (q_{*,s} - p_{*,s})` and a dark-knowledge term over the wrong outputs (Eq. 7).
  - The true-class term "can be rewritten as" `(1/b) sum_s (q_{*,s} - p_{*,s} y_{*,s})` (Eq. 8). This "allows the interpretation of the output of the teacher p_* as a weighting factor of the original ground truth label". Samples with lower teacher confidence "have their gradients rescaled by a factor p_{*,s}". The paper relates this to importance weighting with weights `p_{*,s}/sum_u p_{*,u}` (Eq. 9).
  - From this they build CWTM (CE weighted by the teacher's max probability, "even if the teacher [is] wrong") and DKPP as controls.
  - The abstract-level claim: "the gradient induced by KD can be decomposed into two terms: a dark knowledge term ... and a ground-truth component which corresponds to a simple rescaling of the original gradient that would be obtained using the real labels." — [Furlanello et al., arXiv/ICML 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04770)
- **Tang et al., "Understanding and Improving Knowledge Distillation" (arXiv:2002.03532; Semantic Scholar lists no archival venue).**
  - The KD logit gradient is `(1-lambda)(q_i - y_i) + (lambda/T)(q~_i - p~_i)` (Eq. 2).
  - The paper defines the ratio to the CE gradient, `omega_i = d_i^KD / d_i = (1-lambda) + (lambda/T)(q~_i - p~_i)/(q_i - y_i)` (Eq. 3), and says "KD performs gradient rescaling in the logits space based on teacher model's prediction confidence p_t on the ground-truth class".
  - Proposition 2 gives `E[d_t^KD/d_t] = (1-lambda) + (lambda/T) c~_t/(1 - q_t)`, assuming the teacher's relative confidence `c~_t > 0`, that is, the teacher is more confident on the true class than the student.
  - The paper corrects BAN: the weight "is associated with teacher's confidence on ground-truth, instead of the largest value. Once the teacher makes a wrong prediction, using the largest value would yield contradictory result."
  - On the binary case: "It is also trivial to show that when we have two classes, omega_{i≠t} = omega_t, the primary effect of KD is gradient rescaling. So we can regard the use of KD on binary classification ... as taking the binary log-loss, and multiply with the weight omega_t." — [Tang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03532); venue status per [Semantic Scholar batch API record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/arXiv:2002.03532)
- **Kim et al., PS-KD (ICCV 2021).** This work is already in the bib as `kim2021pskd`, but it is cited only as a self-distillation example.
  - The gradient is `d_i^{KD,t} = (1-alpha)(p_{t,i} - y_i) + alpha(p_{t,i} - p_{t-1,i})` (Eq. 7). For the ground-truth logit this becomes `(p_{t,GT} - 1) - alpha(p_{t-1,GT} - 1)` (Eq. 8).
  - Under the condition `alpha <= min_i p_{t,i}/p_{t-1,i}`, the ratio of L1 gradient norms is `1 - alpha * ((1 - p_{t-1,GT})/(1 - p_{t,GT})) = 1 - alpha * (gamma_{t-1}/gamma_t)` (Eq. 11), where "gamma represents the probability of being incorrect".
  - The paper assumes the current model is better than its past self, `p_{t,GT} >= p_{t-1,GT}`. It reads the factor as implicit hard-example mining "motivated by Proposition 2 in Tang et al." — [Kim et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12000); [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/papers/Kim_Self-Knowledge_Distillation_With_Progressive_Refinement_of_Targets_ICCV_2021_paper.pdf)
- **Zhou et al., "Rethinking Soft Labels for Knowledge Distillation: A Bias-Variance Tradeoff Perspective" (ICLR 2021; header "Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2021").**
  - For the ground-truth logit the paper analyses `d(L_kd - L_ce)/dz_i`, which at tau = 1 "becomes y_i - y^t_{i,1}" (Eq. 4), and observes that `dL_ce/dz_i` and `d(L_kd - L_ce)/dz_i` "always have different signs, leading to a tradeoff".
  - Writing `a = dL_ce/dz_i` and `b = d(L_kd - L_ce)/dz_i`, a sample with b > a is a "regularization sample", because "the variance dominates the optimization direction".
  - The paper proposes weighted soft labels, `L_wsl = (1 - exp(-log y^s_{i,1} / log y^t_{i,1})) L_kd = (1 - exp(-L_ce^s/L_ce^t)) L_kd` (Eq. 5): "if compared to the teacher, a student network is relatively better trained on a sample ... a smaller weight is assigned". — [Zhou et al., arXiv/ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00650)
- **Zhao et al., DKD (CVPR 2022).** The KD loss splits as `KD = TCKD + (1 - p_t^T) NCKD` (Eq. 6). TCKD "transfers knowledge via binary logit distillation" over [target, all non-target]. "A reasonable hypothesis is that TCKD transfers knowledge about the 'difficulty' of training samples." — [Zhao et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08679)
- **Yuan et al. (CVPR 2020).** Shows `L_KD = (1-alpha)H(q,p) + alpha H(p^t_tau, p_tau)`. At tau = 1, `L_KD = H(q~^t, p)` with `q~^t = (1-alpha)q + alpha p^t` (Eq. 6): KD is CE against a learned label-smoothed target. This is a loss-level identity; the paper gives no per-example gradient-reweighting analysis (no "gradient" passages in the text). — [Yuan et al., arXiv (journal ref CVPR 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11723)
- **Zhang & Sabuncu (NeurIPS 2020).** Interprets self-distillation as instance-specific label smoothing (MAP view) and stresses predictive diversity. Its related work credits Furlanello et al. with having "examined the effect of distillation by comparing the gradients of the distillation loss against that of the standard cross-entropy loss with ground truth labels". — [Zhang & Sabuncu, arXiv (NeurIPS 2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05065)
- **Mobahi et al. (NeurIPS 2020).** Analyses self-distillation as kernel regression in Hilbert space: iterations "modify regularization by progressively limiting the number of basis functions". There is no ground-truth gradient decomposition. Its related work states "[Furlanello et al., 2018] interprets dark knowledge as importance weighting." This is the source of the phrase "dark knowledge as importance weighting". — [Mobahi et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05715)
- **Menon et al., "A statistical perspective on distillation" (ICML 2021, PMLR 139).** A Bayes teacher lowers the variance of the student objective, which gives a bias-variance tradeoff. Its related work says teacher probabilities plausibly aid the student by "providing for example a weighting on the samples [Furlanello et al., 2018, Tang et al., 2020]", and that the softmax-CE distillation gradient is "the difference in the probability estimates". — [Menon et al., PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/menon21a.html); [arXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10419)
- **Phuong & Lampert (ICML 2019, PMLR 97).** Studies linear and deep linear classifiers. It proves a generalization bound and names three factors, data geometry, optimization bias and strong monotonicity, with no gradient-reweighting decomposition. — [Phuong & Lampert, PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/phuong19a.html). Menon et al. describe it as assuming "a deep linear model for binary classification". — [Menon et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10419)

### Inferences
- **The match to Tang.** FCT's Prop. 1 combined with Eq. (combined) is Tang et al.'s Eq. 3 at T = 1, written for the single logit of a sigmoid output: `omega = (1-lambda) + lambda (p_s - p_t)/(p_s - y) = (1-lambda) + lambda (1 - e_t/e_s)`. Tang already states that in the two-class case this ratio is the whole effect of KD.
- **The match to PS-KD.** PS-KD's Eq. 11 is literally FCT's `beta + gamma*omega`. With `beta = 1 - alpha` and `gamma = alpha`, `(1-alpha) + alpha(1 - e_t/e_s) = 1 - alpha e_t/e_s`, where `e = gamma_PS-KD = 1 - p_GT`. In general, `beta + gamma*omega = (beta+gamma)(1 - (gamma/(beta+gamma)) e_t/e_s)`.
- **What FCT adds over these.** In multiclass softmax, PS-KD's Eq. 11 holds only for L1 norms and under a condition on the non-target classes, and Tang's result holds only in expectation or per coordinate. In the binary, per-pixel sigmoid case it is exact with no condition. That is the only technical difference.
- **Binary KD is pure TCKD.** A binary per-pixel output has no non-target classes, so the soft BCE toward a detached target is DKD's TCKD alone. That is why the consistency gradient is exactly parallel to the supervised gradient and carries no "dark knowledge" in Hinton's or DKD's sense. This gives a clean, citable way to say why the binary case is special.
- **BAN's reading is an approximation.** BAN writes the factor as `p_*`, which is always non-negative. The exact ratio is `1 - e_t/e_s`, which is negative wherever the student is more confident on the true class than the teacher. The sign reversal is visible in Tang's Eq. 3 and Zhou's Eq. 4, but Tang assumes it away (`c~_t > 0`). PS-KD's setting, a teacher worse than the student, sits in the regime where the consistency part opposes supervision; PS-KD keeps the total factor positive by keeping alpha small.
- **The loss-level form.** Per pixel, `beta*l(z; y) + gamma*l(z; p_t) = (beta+gamma)*l(z; q_bar) + const`, with `q_bar = (beta*y + gamma*p_t)/(beta+gamma)`, which is Yuan et al.'s mixed target. Its gradient `(beta+gamma)(p_s - q_bar)` equals `(beta + gamma*omega)(p_s - y)`. So the "adaptive label smoothing" reading (Yuan) and the "reweighting" reading (Tang, PS-KD) are the same statement, and the paper can mention both.

### Gaps
- I did not find a peer-reviewed venue for Tang et al. Semantic Scholar lists only arXiv, and arXiv v2 is dated Feb 2021. It should be cited as an arXiv preprint, which the bib already does.
- I did not check whether Anil et al. 2018 (codistillation), which Tang cites for binary KD, writes the binary form itself.
- My search did not cover non-English or workshop literature.

## Q2. Has anyone written the binary/per-pixel form (1 - e_t/e_s)(p_s - y), or "the pull helps exactly when the target is closer to the label"? Is it trivial or noteworthy?

### Takeaway
In substance, yes:
- Tang et al. state the binary case explicitly: KD is binary log-loss times omega_t. They call it "trivial to show".
- PS-KD prints the factor `1 - alpha * (error_teacher / error_student)`.
- Zhou et al. define "regularization samples" by the sign condition on the label logit. At tau = 1 this is exactly "teacher's true-class probability below the student's", that is, e_t > e_s.
- Kang et al. (DHO, arXiv 2025; v3 states acceptance at NeurIPS 2026) show that the CE and KD gradient inner product `(p_hat - y)^T (p_hat - p)` is negative exactly when the student lies inside the ball with diameter [y, p]. In one dimension this is FCT's "student closer to the label than the target".

I found no paper that writes the identity per pixel for multi-view/transformation consistency in fully supervised segmentation. The algebra is two lines; reviewers who know KD will recognize it, so it is noteworthy only as the design rationale for a label-supervised consistency loss.

### Cited Findings
- Tang et al.: "It is also trivial to show that when we have two classes, omega_{i≠t} = omega_t, the primary effect of KD is gradient rescaling. So we can regard the use of KD on binary classification ... as taking the binary log-loss, and multiply with the weight omega_t." The ratio is defined as `omega_i = (1-lambda) + (lambda/T)(q~_i - p~_i)/(q_i - y_i)`. — [Tang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03532)
- PS-KD: the rescaling factor is `1 - alpha((1 - p_{t-1,GT})/(1 - p_{t,GT}))`, with `gamma = 1 - p_GT` "the probability of being incorrect" (Eq. 11). The authors assume the past prediction is worse, `p_{t,GT} >= p_{t-1,GT}`. — [Kim et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.12000)
- Zhou et al.: on the label logit, the CE gradient and the KD-minus-CE gradient "always have different signs". Samples where the latter dominates are "regularization samples". At tau = 1 the comparison is between `y^s_{i,1}` and `y^t_{i,1}`, the student's and teacher's true-class probabilities. — [Zhou et al., arXiv/ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00650)
- Kang et al. (DHO), "Simple yet Effective Semi-supervised Knowledge Distillation from Vision-Language Models via Dual-Head Optimization" (arXiv:2505.07675; v3 comment "Accepted at NeurIPS 2026").
  - The classifier-weight gradients are `(p_hat - y)z^T` and `(p_hat - p)z^T`, so their inner product is `(p_hat - y)^T (p_hat - p) ||z||^2`.
  - Proposition 1: this term equals `||p_hat - m||^2 - r^2`, with `m = (y+p)/2` and `r = ||y - p||/2`, "so gradient conflict is guaranteed whenever p_hat lies inside the ball B(m, r) with the segment [y, p] as its diameter".
  - Corollary 1 covers interpolation. The paper measures negative cosine between CE and KD gradients in practice. — [Kang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07675)
- Furlanello et al. gave the first "rescaling of the ground-truth gradient" reading, though with a non-negative weight `p_*`. — [Furlanello et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04770)
- Nagarajan et al. (NeurIPS 2023 per Semantic Scholar): distilled students "systematically exaggerat[e] the confidence levels of the teacher", even in self-distillation. In practice, then, the student often leads its teacher on training data, which is the regime where the pull opposes supervision. — [Nagarajan et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12923)
- Closest segmentation-specific item found: Wang et al. (MICCAI 2024) build a mask of the teacher's false pixels from the ground-truth mask and correct the soft labels there. They correct the target per pixel; they do not derive a gradient identity. — [Wang et al., MICCAI 2024](https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2024/paper/2751_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- **The identity is correct.**
  - For `y ∈ {0,1}`, `p - y = (1-2y)|p - y|`. So `p_s - p_t = (1-2y)(e_s - e_t)`, and dividing by `p_s - y = (1-2y)e_s` gives `1 - e_t/e_s`.
  - The first-order error change `-eta*sigma'(z_s)*(e_s - e_t)` in analysis.tex is also correct, since `(1-2y)^2 = 1`.
  - The leading-pixel bound `w*(e^o - e^k) <= (1 - 2e^o)e^o <= 1/8` holds; the maximum is at `e^o = 1/4`.
  - I checked these numerically over 2×10^5 random (y, z_s, z_t). The identity error was at most 2.2e-16. The descent direction matched the sign rule in every case. The largest leading-pixel movement under the FCT weight was 0.1248, against a bound of 0.125. Tang's ratio at T = 1 equals `(1-lambda) + lambda*omega`. PS-KD's factor equals `1 - alpha*e_t/e_s`. DHO's ball condition reduces in 1-D to `e_s < e_t`.
- **Scope of exactness.** The identity is exact only for one sigmoid logit per pixel. With softmax multiclass segmentation, `p_s - p_t` is not parallel to `p_s - y`: the non-target coordinates carry NCKD. Only the target coordinate, or the binary target-vs-rest collapse, obeys the identity. The paper should say "binary" wherever it says "consistency is reweighted supervision".
- **Sign rule vs ratio.**
  - The sign rule (Corollary 1) holds for any consistency loss whose logit gradient has the sign of `p_s - p_t`: soft BCE (equivalently forward KL with a detached target), squared error on probabilities, and squared error on logits (since sigma is monotone).
  - The exact ratio `1 - e_t/e_s` is specific to BCE, and to probability MSE in p-space.
  - Neither holds for a non-detached, symmetric consistency (Pi-model, R-Drop style), because then gradient also flows into the target view.
- **The weight is unbounded.** `omega = 1 - e_t/e_s` goes to minus infinity as `e_s` goes to 0, but the gradient `(1-2y)(e_s - e_t)` stays in [-1, 1]. Presenting the effect as a "weight" can mislead about magnitude. The "movement" `e_s - e_t` that the draft already uses is the better quantity to stress.
- **Per-pixel parallel does not mean parameter-space aligned.** The identity makes each pixel's logit-space gradient parallel to its supervised gradient. The parameter-space gradients are `sum_i omega_i a_i J_i` and `sum_i a_i J_i`, and their cosine depends on cross-pixel terms `J_i^T J_j`. Even with all `omega_i >= 0` the parameter-space cosine can be negative. Two-pixel example: `g_1 = (1,0)`, `g_2 = (-0.9, 0.1)`, `omega = (0, 1)` gives a negative inner product with `g_1 + g_2`. So the measured cosine (about 0.5 with weights, about 0.2 without) is an empirical observation that the theory makes plausible, not a consequence of Prop. 1. The paper should not present it as a verification of the identity. DHO's Eq. 4 (a PSD Gram matrix for one example) shows the same structure.
- **"Brings no information beyond the label" needs nuance.** The consistency term adds no target other than the label. What it adds is a weighting of the label's gradient that depends on another view's prediction; PS-KD reads the same thing as implicit hard-example mining. Suggested wording: "adds no target beyond the label; it reweights the label's gradient by how the two views' errors compare."
- **Trivial or noteworthy.** The algebra is trivial, and Tang et al. call the binary case trivial. The multiclass true-class version appears in at least three well-cited KD papers (BAN 2018, Tang 2020, PS-KD 2021), and the sign condition in two more (Zhou 2021, DHO 2025). A KD-literate reviewer is likely to treat an uncredited "Proposition" as overclaiming. Credited, it is a correct and useful lens, because the label decides per pixel where the pull helps or hurts, which semi-supervised consistency cannot know. The design that follows from it (the asymmetric threshold-derived weight and the bidirectional gate) is where FCT can claim novelty.

### Gaps
- My searches (KD gradient rescaling, segmentation KD pixel weighting, self-distillation error ratio) found no paper that writes `(1 - e_t/e_s)(p_s - y)` per pixel for transformation or multi-view consistency in segmentation. Absence in a limited search is not proof; the parallel researchers covering multi-view consistency may find one.
- DHO's NeurIPS 2026 acceptance comes from its arXiv v3 comment (24 Oct 2026). I could not check it against the proceedings, which are not yet published.

## Q3. Do methods gate distillation or consistency by the sign of e_t - e_s, or measure gradient alignment (cosine) between an auxiliary loss and the main loss?

### Takeaway
Yes, both exist.
- **Label-aware gating or weighting** that compares teacher and student against the label is established in KD. Examples: Zhou et al.'s weights `1 - exp(-L_ce^s/L_ce^t)` and their hard-exclusion experiment; KDCL-Naive, which uses as target the peer with the smallest CE to the label; conditional teacher-student learning, which gates by teacher correctness; TGeo-KD, which learns a fusion ratio from student-teacher-ground-truth geometry; Hard-Gate KD, which switches between teacher and data; and CA-MKD (already cited).
- **Gradient-cosine diagnostics and gates** are standard: Du et al. use an auxiliary gradient only when its cosine with the main gradient is non-negative; PCGrad projects conflicting gradients; GradVac and OL-AUX weight by gradient similarity; Dery et al. decompose auxiliary updates into help, hurt and neutral directions; POS (ICCV 2025) reports negative cosine between supervised and consistency gradients in semi-supervised segmentation; DHO reports it between CE and KD.

I found no method that applies the per-pixel sign rule to multi-view consistency under full supervision. But the draft's "Of these rules, only FCT compares the two errors" is too strong as written.

### Cited Findings
- **Zhou et al. (ICLR 2021), weighted soft labels.**
  - The weight `1 - exp(-L_ce^s/L_ce^t)` lowers the KD weight where the student is better than the teacher on the ground truth.
  - The paper also runs a hard gate, "Excluding regularization samples": `L_ce` alone where a < b, and `L_ce + L_kd` where a >= b. It reaches 74.59% on CIFAR-100 (teacher WRN-40-2 at 75.61%, student WRN-16-2, tau = 4; the pair is stated in the Table 1 caption). Standard KD reaches 74.92% and direct training 73.26%, so the hard gate is -0.33 vs KD.
  - "Only on regularization samples" reaches 73.86%. Weighted soft labels improve both subsets (Table 5). — [Zhou et al., arXiv/ICLR 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00650)
- **Guo et al., KDCL (CVPR 2020).** Online collaborative distillation: "The raw images are augmented separately with different random seeds, and the soft target is generated to supervise all networks". The loss is `L = sum_i L_CE^i + lambda L_KD^i`. KDCL-Naive picks "the logit with the smallest cross-entropy loss among all students", `z_t = z_k, k = argmin_i L_CE(z_i, y)` (Eq. 4). KDCL-MinLogit builds the target from element-wise minima of the target-normalised logits. — [Guo et al., CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Guo_Online_Knowledge_Distillation_via_Collaborative_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf)
- **Meng et al., Conditional Teacher-Student Learning (ICASSP 2019).** The student "selectively chooses to learn from either the teacher model or the ground truth labels conditioned on whether the teacher can correctly predict the ground truth". The gate judges the target alone. — [Meng et al., arXiv (journal ref ICASSP 2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12399)
- **Hu et al., TGeo-KD (ICLR 2024 poster).** Learns "a sample-wise knowledge fusion ratio, exploiting both the correctness of teacher and student, as well as how well the student mimics the teacher on each sample", from student-teacher-ground-truth "trilateral geometric relations", by bilevel optimisation. — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=OZitfSXpdT); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15112)
- **Lee et al., Hard Gate KD (EMNLP 2022 per arXiv comment).** "A hard gate knowledge distillation scheme that switches between learning from a teacher model and training data", using the teacher "as a gauge to detect miscalibration of a student". — [Lee et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12427)
- **Du et al., "Adapting Auxiliary Losses Using Gradient Similarity" (arXiv:1812.02224; Semantic Scholar lists no archival venue).** The update is `theta <- theta - alpha(G + V max(0, cos(G, V)))`, with G the main and V the auxiliary gradient (Eq. 3). An unweighted variant uses V if and only if `cos(G, V) > 0`. The authors note that cosine "is not a silver bullet that guarantees positive transfer" and is "a local quantity". — [Du et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.02224)
- **Yu et al., PCGrad (NeurIPS 2020 per Semantic Scholar).** "Projects a task's gradient onto the normal plane of the gradient of any other task that has a conflicting gradient." — [Yu et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.06782)
- **Wang et al., Gradient Vaccine (ICLR; Semantic Scholar lists ICLR, arXiv Oct 2020, hence ICLR 2021).** "Gradient similarity measured along the optimization trajectory is an important signal". The method encourages "geometrically aligned parameter updates". — [Wang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.05874)
- **Lin et al., OL-AUX (NeurIPS 2019).** Combines auxiliary tasks online on "the idea that auxiliary tasks should provide gradient directions that, in the long term, help to decrease the loss of the main task". — [Lin et al., NeurIPS proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/hash/0e900ad84f63618452210ab8baae0218-Abstract.html)
- **Dery et al. (ICLR 2021 poster).** "Decompose auxiliary updates into directions which help, damage or leave the primary task loss unchanged." — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=1GTma8HwlYp); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11346)
- **Sun et al., "Two Losses, One Goal: Balancing Conflict Gradients for Semi-supervised Semantic Segmentation" (ICCV 2025).** Figure 1(a) shows "the negative cosine similarity between gradients derived from supervised and unsupervised losses" on Pascal (366 labels). The proposed POS keeps the combined gradient at non-negative cosine with both. — [Sun et al., CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Sun_Two_Losses_One_Goal_Balancing_Conflict_Gradients_for_Semi-supervised_Semantic_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)
- **Kang et al., DHO.** `CosSim(grad L_CE, grad L_KD)` turns negative for both the classifier and the feature extractor. The proposed fix uses two heads. — [Kang et al., arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07675)
- **Background, multi-task gradient balancing:** MGDA (Sener & Koltun, NeurIPS 2018), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04650); GradNorm (ICML 2018 per arXiv journal ref), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.02257); CAGrad (NeurIPS 2021 per Semantic Scholar), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14048).

### Inferences
- **WSLD is the closest precedent for FCT's label-aware weight w.** Both downweight distillation where the student already beats the target with respect to the label, and keep full weight where it lags. The differences:
  1. WSLD's unit is the sample and its signal is the CE ratio; FCT's unit is the pixel and its signal is the error comparison.
  2. WSLD's weight is a smooth `1 - exp(-L_s/L_t)`. FCT's is `w = 1` on lag and `(1 - 2e_t)_+` on lead, derived from the decision threshold, with a guarantee: no pull toward a wrong target, and the harmful movement is bounded by 1/8.
  3. WSLD scales the whole multiclass KD loss, including NCKD; in FCT's binary case there is no NCKD.
- **FCT's upright gate has precedents.** `g = 1[e* < e^o]` together with "pull toward the flipped view closest to the label" is the per-pixel analogue of WSLD's hard "excluding regularization samples" and of KDCL-Naive's min-CE target selection. KDCL also uses separately augmented inputs, though with different networks, not views of one network.
- **WSLD's hard gate lost to plain KD (-0.33).** This is a useful caution: removing all leading samples is not obviously best. In multiclass KD it also discards those samples' dark knowledge; binary FCT has none, so the tradeoff differs. FCT's choice of a margin weight over `w = 0` on leading pixels is still well motivated, and the paper's ablation of weight rules is the right evidence to point to.
- **The cosine measurement is an established diagnostic.** Reporting the cosine between the consistency and supervised gradients follows Du et al., PCGrad and GradVac, and the two most relevant recent reports (POS in semi-supervised segmentation, DHO for CE vs KD) find negative cosine. FCT's positive cosine, raised from about 0.2 to about 0.5 by the label-aware weights, fits that literature: the weights remove the per-pixel component that the identity predicts to be in conflict. FCT should cite these for the measurement and not present the measurement itself as new.
- **What remains unoccupied** in the searched literature is the combination: per-pixel, binary, transformation (flip) views of one network, full supervision, an asymmetric threshold-derived margin weight, and a bidirectional "more-accurate view teaches" gate. That is a design contribution, not a theoretical one.
